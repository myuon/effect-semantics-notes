# Principal-word recovery v1

## Status

**Exact criterion and useful sufficient conditions for recovering the original
single-word handler rule from trace-language semantics.**

The general sound rule is

$$
L\longmapsto\Phi_{\Delta,K}(L).
$$

This page asks when, for principal bounds, it can be widened to the simpler
displayed word

$$
b\Delta e\longmapsto bke.
$$

## 1. Setup

Let

$$
L=J(b\Delta e),
\qquad
K\subseteq J(k),
$$

where $b\in B$, $e\in M=B*\mathcal D^*$, and the common clause bound satisfies

$$
1\preceq k.
\tag{OptionalClause}
$$

The desired principal recovery property is

$$
\boxed{
\Phi_{\Delta,K}(J(b\Delta e))
\subseteq
J(bke).
}
\tag{PWR}
$$

If PWR holds, FE-014 gives the old-looking type

$$
H_\Delta:
\mathsf{GTree}_{J(b\Delta e)}X
\to
\mathsf{GTree}_{J(bke)}X.
$$

This is a derived widening of the trace-language rule, not the definition of
the handler.

## 2. Deletion characterization of the preorder

Write words in alternating normal form. The preorder on $M$ is generated by
lowering base factors and inserting optional interface tokens.

### Lemma PW-001 — Subword characterization

For $m,n\in M$,

$$
m\preceq n
$$

iff the normal form of $m$ can be obtained from that of $n$ by:

1. deleting zero or more free-interface factors;
2. multiplying adjacent base factors exposed by those deletions;
3. lowering the resulting base factors using $\leq_B$.

### Proof

Each generating inequality $1\preceq\Gamma$ inserts one interface token;
reading a derivation downward deletes it. Monoidal closure permits insertion in
any context, and the embedded base preorder gives step 3. Conversely, each
listed edit is generated by those rules. Uniqueness of free-product normal form
completes the induction on edits. $\square$

This lemma makes precise what an upper-bound word permits at runtime: any
subsequence of its optional free tokens may be the actual trace.

## 3. The three runtime cases under a principal bound

Take an actual trace

$$
m\preceq b\Delta e.
$$

There are three possibilities.

### The displayed $\Delta$ survives

Then it is the first free factor of $m$, so

$$
m=b'\Delta e',
\qquad
b'\preceq b,quad e'\preceq e.
$$

The handler produces traces $b'k'e'$ with $k'\in K$, and therefore

$$
b'k'e'\preceq bke.
$$

This case is always safe.

### The displayed $\Delta$ is deleted and no later matching $\Delta$ is exposed

The actual trace is base-only or its first retained free factor is another
interface. The handler leaves it unchanged. Such a trace satisfies

$$
m\preceq be\preceq bke,
$$

where the second inequality uses `OptionalClause`. This case is also safe.

### The displayed $\Delta$ is deleted and a later $\Delta$ is exposed

After deleting zero or more earlier tokens from $e$, suppose the retained
residual begins

$$
a\Delta t,
\qquad a\in B.
$$

The handler processes this **later actual** $\Delta$, producing

$$
bak't.
$$

The simple target instead places $k$ at the deleted displayed position:

$$
bke.
$$

There is no general noncommutative inequality between these words. This is the
only new obstruction.

## 4. Exact anchoring criterion

Define the possible later matching heads of $e$ by

$$
\mathsf{Later}_\Delta(e)
=
\{(a,t)\mid a\in B,\ a\Delta t\preceq e\}.
$$

The relation $a\Delta t\preceq e$ permits deletion of earlier optional
interfaces of $e$ until a $\Delta$ becomes the first retained free factor.

### Theorem PW-002 — Principal recovery criterion

Under $K\subseteq J(k)$ and $1\preceq k$, PWR holds iff

$$
\forall(a,t)\in\mathsf{Later}_\Delta(e).\;
\forall k'\in K.\;
bak't\preceq bke.
\tag{Anchor}
$$

If $K=J(k)$, it is sufficient and necessary to check the maximal replacement:

$$
\forall(a,t)\in\mathsf{Later}_\Delta(e).\;
bakt\preceq bke.
\tag{PrincipalAnchor}
$$

### Proof

For sufficiency, take $m\preceq b\Delta e$ and use the three cases of Section
3. The first two land in $J(bke)$ automatically. In the third case, the output
is below one of the words in `Anchor` after possible lowering of $b,a,k',t$.

For necessity, take any $(a,t)\in\mathsf{Later}_\Delta(e)$. Deleting the
displayed token gives the permitted actual trace

$$
ba\Delta t\preceq be\preceq b\Delta e.
$$

Handling it yields $bak't$ for every replacement choice $k'\in K$. PWR requires
each such word to lie below $bke$. For $K=J(k)$, monotonicity makes checking
$k'=k$ imply all $k'\preceq k$, while necessity includes $k$ itself. $\square$

The criterion is exact for the grade transformer. A concrete clause may realize
fewer traces than its declared $K$, in which case its concrete tree can admit a
tighter bound.

## 5. The clean sufficient condition

Say $e$ is **$\Delta$-free** when its normal form contains no $\Delta$ token.

### Corollary PW-003 — No repeated target interface

If $e$ is $\Delta$-free, then

$$
\Phi_{\Delta,K}(J(b\Delta e))
\subseteq
J(bke).
$$

### Proof

$\mathsf{Later}_\Delta(e)=\varnothing$, so `Anchor` is vacuous. $\square$

Notice that $e$ may contain other free interfaces. If the displayed $\Delta$
is skipped, the handler stops at the first retained other-interface request and
leaves the trace unchanged; `OptionalClause` then supplies the widening into
$bke$.

This condition is substantially weaker than requiring a base-only tail.

## 6. Exact-layer sufficient condition

If the typing judgement guarantees that the displayed $\Delta$ actually occurs
and cannot be deleted, only the first case of Section 3 is possible.

### Corollary PW-004 — Mandatory displayed head

In the exact-effect variant without the coercion $be\preceq b\Delta e$ at this
position,

$$
b\Delta e\longmapsto bke
$$

is sound for arbitrary $e$.

This explains why the original word rule looked natural under a positional or
exact-layer reading, even though the original system also intended optional
insertion.

## 7. Repeated-interface counterexample

### Proposition PW-005 — Repeated optional target obstruction

Let $w\in B$ be a noncommutative base effect relative to the clause effect $k$,
and take

$$
b=1,
\qquad
e=w\Delta.
$$

The declared input bound is

$$
\Delta w\Delta.
$$

Delete the displayed first $\Delta$. The actual trace

$$
w\Delta
$$

is permitted. The runtime handler skips across the base prefix $w$, processes
the later actual $\Delta$, and produces

$$
wk.
$$

The principal rule claims the bound

$$
kw\Delta.
$$

Soundness would require

$$
wk\preceq kw\Delta,
$$

which does not follow from the preordered-monoid laws. Thus PWR fails in the
free model whenever these two words remain incomparable.

This is the minimal form of the earlier optional-bound obstruction.

## 8. Other ways to satisfy the anchor condition

Repeated $\Delta$ tokens do not force failure; they create proof obligations.
`PrincipalAnchor` can still hold if the effect algebra provides suitable laws,
for example:

- the relevant skipped prefix $a$ commutes with $k$;
- $k$ absorbs $a$ and the later $\Delta$;
- the preorder explicitly validates

  $$
  akt\preceq ke
  $$

  for every possible later matching residual;
- a refined type system excludes precisely the unsafe deletion paths.

These are additional interaction laws, not consequences of graded monad
structure.

## 9. Relation to the original thesis rule

The original target

$$
\widehat T_{b\Delta e}A
\longrightarrow
\widehat T_{bke}A
$$

is therefore closest to one of these readings:

1. the displayed $\Delta$ is an anchored positional layer;
2. the residual $e$ contains no later occurrence of the handled interface;
3. the model assumes `PrincipalAnchor` as an interaction law;
4. $bke$ is an asserted coarse bound whose required inequality was left
   implicit.

For the intended first-actual-free-head runtime semantics, the universally
sound rule remains

$$
L\longmapsto\Phi_{\Delta,K}(L).
$$

The principal rule should be offered as a derived rule when PW-002 can be
proved, not built into the core semantics.

## 10. Practical typing rule

A surface type checker may implement:

$$
\frac{
M:A!J(b\Delta e)
\quad H:\Delta\Rightarrow J(k)
\quad 1\preceq k
\quad \mathsf{Anchor}(b,\Delta,e,k)
}{
\mathsf{handle}_\Delta M\;\mathsf{with}\;H
:A!J(bke)
}.
$$

When the anchor check fails, the program remains typable using the general
language grade

$$
\Phi_{\Delta,J(k)}(J(b\Delta e)).
$$

Thus precision failure does not become program rejection; it selects the more
general effect representation.
