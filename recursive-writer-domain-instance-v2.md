# Recursive Writer domain instance v2

## Status

**Concrete standard domain category sufficient for the adequacy construction.**
This replaces the placeholder “choose a suitable CPO category.”

## 1. Size restriction for the first theorem

For the first recursive Writer theorem, assume:

- the Writer monoid $W$ is countable;
- primitive value domains are countably based;
- each interface has finitely or countably many operations;
- parameter and response types are generated by the fixed type grammar and are
  countably based.

This covers the intended examples with finite strings, natural numbers,
booleans, products, sums, and effectful functions.  Larger universes can be
treated later with a correspondingly larger category of algebraic domains.

## 2. Predomains and computations

Use a standard cartesian closed category of countably based bifinite predomains
and continuous maps for value types.  It supports:

- finite products;
- separated/disjoint sums;
- continuous-function spaces;
- lifting $D\mapsto D_\bot$.

Computation domains are pointed through lifting or through the recursively
defined pointed Writer carrier.  Least fixed points are taken only in pointed
computation/function spaces.

This separation avoids the fixed-point/coproduct collapse.  We do not equip
every value-domain endomorphism with a fixed point; disjoint sums live in the
predomain category, while unguarded recursion is interpreted in designated
pointed computation spaces.

The closure properties and inverse-limit treatment of recursive equations are
standard domain theory; see Abramsky and Jung, [Domain Theory, Chapters 4--5](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/298/handbook.pdf).

## 3. The Writer endofunctor

For fixed $A$ and row $\rho$, define

$$
F_{A,\rho}(X)
=
\left(
W\times
\left(
D_A+
\sum_{\Delta\in\rho}
\sum_i
D_{P_i}\times[D_{R_i}\to_c X]
\right)
\right)_\bot.
$$

Every constructor used here is locally continuous.  Although function space is
contravariant in its domain, $D_{R_i}$ is fixed; as a functor of the recursive
variable $X$, $[D_{R_i}\to_c X]$ is covariant and locally continuous.

Thus $F_{A,\rho}$ is a locally continuous endofunctor on the chosen embedding--
projection category.

## 4. Bilimit solution

Start from the least pointed domain and iterate:

$$
D_0=\{\bot\},
\qquad
D_{n+1}=F_{A,\rho}(D_n).
$$

The connecting maps form embedding--projection pairs.  Their bilimit $D_\infty$
has an isomorphism

$$
D_\infty\cong F_{A,\rho}(D_\infty).
$$

This is the selected

$$
\mathsf{CWTree}_\rho A.
$$

The general categorical basis is the Smyth--Plotkin limit--colimit coincidence
for recursive domain equations; see [The Category-Theoretic Solution of
Recursive Domain Equations](https://doi.org/10.1137/0211062).

:::{prf:theorem} Existence of the recursive Writer domain
:label: thm-recursive-writer-domain-existence-v2

Under the countable-domain assumptions above, the recursive Writer equation has
a canonical bilimit solution.  `prefix`, bind, row weakening, the term
interpretation, and the handler functional are continuous maps on this
solution.
:::

:::{prf:proof}
Closure of bifinite/countably based domains under the displayed constructors
keeps every stage in the category.  Local continuity preserves the bilimit and
produces the fixed-point isomorphism.  Each semantic operation is defined from
continuous constructors, case analysis, evaluation, composition, and least
fixed point; structural induction on syntax proves continuity of term and
clause interpretations.
:::

## 5. Finite projections

The bilimit supplies projections

$$
\pi_n:D_\infty\to D_n
$$

and embeddings

$$
\iota_n:D_n\to D_\infty
$$

such that

$$
\bigsqcup_n\iota_n\pi_n=\mathsf{id}_{D_\infty}.
$$

Therefore equality and the logical relation can be checked through all finite
projections.  Define the coinductive request-continuation clause equivalently by

$$
M\mathcal C_A^\rho t
\iff
\forall n.\ M\mathcal C_{A,n}^\rho\pi_n(t),
$$

where $\mathcal C_{A,n}$ unfolds at most $n$ request observations.

This supplies a concrete construction of the relation that was previously
described as “coinductive, or through all finite observations.”

## 6. Admissibility from projections

Each finite-level predicate is defined by ordinary induction and is admissible:
root information is separated, continuations are checked only one level lower,
and function relations are pointwise.  The full predicate is their countable
intersection.

:::{prf:theorem} Projective admissibility
:label: thm-recursive-writer-projective-admissibility-v2

The value/computation relations constructed from all $\pi_n$ contain bottom
where appropriate and are closed under directed suprema.  They satisfy the
return, request, function, recursion, and handler clauses stated in the logical
relation page.
:::

:::{prf:proof}
Induct on $n$ and value type for the finite predicates.  Directed suprema are
computed pointwise, and continuous projections preserve them.  Intersections
of predicates each containing bottom and closed under directed suprema retain
those properties.  The bilimit identity converts agreement at every projection
into agreement in $D_\infty$.
:::

## 7. Consequence for the adequacy dependency

The former open infrastructure items now reduce to standard closure and bilimit
theorems for the selected domain category.  No separate black-box
computational-adequacy theorem is assumed:

1. the bilimit constructs $\mathsf{CWTree}$;
2. finite projections construct the admissible logical relation;
3. syntax induction proves continuity;
4. the fundamental lemma proves operational reflection.

What remains for a fully formal proof is detailed verification inside one
chosen presentation of bifinite domains, not a missing semantic idea.
