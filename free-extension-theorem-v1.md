# Free extension theorem v1

## Status

**Detailed paper proof of the first main-theorem layer in `Set`.**

This page proves that an ordered algebraic base signature and a family of free
interfaces generate a strong trace-language graded tree monad with exhaustive
one-shot shallow handlers.

## 1. Assumptions

Work in `Set` and fix a universe of small types. Assume:

1. a small preordered monoid

   $$
   (B,\cdot,1,\leq_B);
   $$

2. a small base signature

   $$
   \Sigma_B
   =
   \{\beta:P_\beta\to R_\beta\};
   $$

3. a grade assignment $|\beta|\in B$;
4. a small family $\mathcal D$ of free interfaces, each containing operations

   $$
   \operatorname{op}:P_{\operatorname{op}}
   \to R_{\operatorname{op}}.
   $$

All parameter and result sets are small. No finiteness assumption on result sets
is required. Trees are well founded; denotations of recursion-free source terms
have finite syntactic rank.

## 2. Effect monoid and preorder

Let

$$
M=B*\mathcal D^*
$$

be the coproduct/free product of monoids. Write multiplication by juxtaposition
and unit by $1$.

Define $\preceq$ as the least preorder on $M$ satisfying:

1. if $b\leq_Bb'$, then $b\preceq b'$;
2. $1\preceq\Delta$ for every $\Delta\in\mathcal D$;
3. if $m\preceq m'$ and $n\preceq n'$, then

   $$
   mn\preceq m'n'.
   $$

### Lemma FE-001

$(M,\cdot,1,\preceq)$ is a preordered monoid, and the embedding
$B\to M$ is monotone.

### Proof

Reflexivity and transitivity hold by preorder generation. Compatibility with
multiplication is a generating closure rule. The monoid equations are inherited
from the free product. The first generator gives monotonicity of $B\to M$.
$\square$

## 3. Downset quantale

Let

$$
Q=\mathsf{Down}(M)
$$

be the set of downward-closed subsets of $M$. For $S\subseteq M$, write

$$
\downarrow S
=
\{m\mid\exists s\in S.\ m\preceq s\}.
$$

Define

$$
1_Q=\downarrow\{1\},
$$

$$
L\otimes K
=
\downarrow\{mn\mid m\in L,n\in K\},
$$

and

$$
\bigvee_iL_i=\bigcup_iL_i.
$$

### Lemma FE-002 — Closure calculation

For arbitrary $S,T\subseteq M$,

$$
\downarrow((\downarrow S)(\downarrow T))
=
\downarrow(ST),
$$

where $ST=\{st\mid s\in S,t\in T\}$.

### Proof

The right-to-left inclusion is immediate because
$S\subseteq\downarrow S$ and $T\subseteq\downarrow T$. Conversely, if
$m\preceq s'$ and $s'=uv$ with $u\preceq s\in S$ and $v\preceq t\in T$,
monotonicity gives $uv\preceq st$, hence $m\in\downarrow(ST)$. $\square$

### Theorem FE-003 — Quantale theorem

$(Q,\otimes,1_Q,\subseteq)$ is a unital quantale.

### Proof

For associativity,

$$
(L\otimes K)\otimes N
=
\downarrow\{(mn)p\mid m\in L,n\in K,p\in N\}
$$

by FE-002, which equals $L\otimes(K\otimes N)$ by associativity in $M$.
The same calculation with $1m=m=m1$ proves the unit laws.

Arbitrary unions of downsets are downsets, so $Q$ is a complete lattice. For
left distribution,

$$
L\otimes\bigcup_iK_i
=
\downarrow\bigcup_i\{mn\mid m\in L,n\in K_i\}
=
\bigcup_i(L\otimes K_i).
$$

The last equality holds because a union of downsets is already downward closed.
Right distribution is symmetric. $\square$

Define the principal embedding

$$
J:M\to Q,
\qquad
J(m)=\downarrow\{m\}.
$$

### Corollary FE-004

$J$ is strong monoidal and monotone:

$$
J(1)=1_Q,
\qquad
J(mn)=J(m)\otimes J(n),
$$

$$
m\preceq n\Longrightarrow J(m)\subseteq J(n).
$$

The multiplication equation is FE-002 on singleton sets.

## 4. Combined signature functor

Define the polynomial functor

$$
\Sigma Z
=
\left(
\coprod_{\beta\in\Sigma_B}
P_\beta\times Z^{R_\beta}
\right)
+
\left(
\coprod_{\Gamma\in\mathcal D}
\coprod_{\operatorname{op}\in\Gamma}
P_{\operatorname{op}}\times
Z^{R_{\operatorname{op}}}
\right).
$$

For a set $X$, let $\mathsf{GTree}(X)$ be the well-founded tree set generated by

$$
\mathsf{ret}:X\to\mathsf{GTree}(X),
$$

$$
\mathsf{base}_\beta:
P_\beta\times
\mathsf{GTree}(X)^{R_\beta}
\to\mathsf{GTree}(X),
$$

and

$$
\mathsf{free}_{\Gamma,\operatorname{op}}:
P_{\operatorname{op}}
\times
\mathsf{GTree}(X)^{R_{\operatorname{op}}}
\to\mathsf{GTree}(X).
$$

Equivalently, it is the initial algebra for

$$
Y\mapsto X+\Sigma Y.
$$

## 5. Functor and monad

Define $\mathsf{map}(f)$ by recursion:

$$
\mathsf{map}(f)(\mathsf{ret}(x))
=
\mathsf{ret}(f(x)),
$$

and preserve every operation node while mapping all continuation subtrees.

Define bind by replacing returns:

$$
\mathsf{ret}(x)\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f=f(x),
$$

$$
\mathsf{base}_\beta(p,k)\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f
=
\mathsf{base}_\beta
(p,\lambda r.k(r)\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f),
$$

and analogously for free nodes.

### Lemma FE-005 — Functor laws

$$
\mathsf{map}(\mathsf{id})=\mathsf{id},
$$

$$
\mathsf{map}(g\circ f)
=
\mathsf{map}(g)\circ\mathsf{map}(f).
$$

### Lemma FE-006 — Monad laws

$$
\mathsf{ret}(x)\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f=f(x),
$$

$$
t\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}\mathsf{ret}=t,
$$

$$
(t\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f)
\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}g
=
t\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}
(\lambda x.f(x)\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}g).
$$

### Proofs

Well-founded tree induction. Return cases are definitional. At either operation
node, preserve the root and apply the induction hypotheses pointwise to every
result-indexed subtree. $\square$

## 6. Path grades

For exact statements about bind, a path must remember the value at its return
leaf. Define the set of **value-labelled complete paths** by

$$
\mathsf{LTr}(\mathsf{ret}(x))=\{(1,x)\},
$$

$$
\mathsf{LTr}(\mathsf{base}_\beta(p,k))
=
\bigcup_{r\in R_\beta}
\{(|\beta|m,x)\mid(m,x)\in\mathsf{LTr}(k(r))\},
$$

$$
\mathsf{LTr}
(\mathsf{free}_{\Gamma,\operatorname{op}}(p,k))
=
\bigcup_{r\in R_{\operatorname{op}}}
\{(\Gamma m,x)\mid(m,x)\in\mathsf{LTr}(k(r))\}.
$$

The unlabelled trace set used for grading is its first projection:

$$
\mathsf{Tr}(t)=\{m\mid\exists x.(m,x)\in\mathsf{LTr}(t)\}.
$$

Equivalently, it satisfies

$$
\mathsf{Tr}(\mathsf{ret}(x))=\{1\},
$$

$$
\mathsf{Tr}(\mathsf{base}_\beta(p,k))
=
\bigcup_{r\in R_\beta}
|\beta|\,\mathsf{Tr}(k(r)),
$$

$$
\mathsf{Tr}
(\mathsf{free}_{\Gamma,\operatorname{op}}(p,k))
=
\bigcup_{r\in R_{\operatorname{op}}}
\Gamma\,\mathsf{Tr}(k(r)).
$$

Here $mS=\{ms\mid s\in S\}$.

### Lemma FE-007

Mapping return values does not change traces:

$$
\mathsf{Tr}(\mathsf{map}(f)(t))
=
\mathsf{Tr}(t).
$$

### Lemma FE-008 — Exact bind traces

$$
\mathsf{LTr}(t\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f)
=
\{
(mn,y)
\mid
(m,x)\in\mathsf{LTr}(t),
(n,y)\in\mathsf{LTr}(f(x))
\}.
$$

Consequently, if $\mathsf{Tr}(t)\subseteq L$ and
$\mathsf{Tr}(f(x))\subseteq K$ for every $x$, then

$$
\mathsf{Tr}(t\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}f)
\subseteq L\otimes K.
$$

Both lemmas follow by tree induction.

## 7. Trace-graded carrier

For $L\in Q$, define

$$
\widehat T_LX
=
\{t\in\mathsf{GTree}(X)\mid
\mathsf{Tr}(t)\subseteq L\}.
$$

For $L\subseteq K$, weakening is inclusion

$$
\tau_{L,K}:\widehat T_LX\hookrightarrow\widehat T_KX.
$$

Return and bind restrict by FE-008:

$$
\eta:X\to\widehat T_{1_Q}X,
$$

$$
\mathsf{bind}_{L,K}:
\widehat T_LX
\times
(X\to\widehat T_KY)
\to
\widehat T_{L\otimes K}Y.
$$

### Theorem FE-009 — Graded monad theorem

$L\mapsto\widehat T_L$ is a $Q$-graded monad with coherent proof-irrelevant
subeffecting.

### Proof

Typing of unit and bind is FE-008. Laws are inherited from FE-006 because all
maps are restrictions of the same underlying tree functions. For inclusions
$L\subseteq K\subseteq N$,

$$
\tau_{K,N}\circ\tau_{L,K}=\tau_{L,N}
$$

holds as equality of inclusion functions. Compatibility of weakening with bind
is equality of the same underlying bound tree. $\square$

## 8. Strength

Define

$$
\mathsf{st}_{A,L}:
A\times\widehat T_LX
\to
\widehat T_L(A\times X)
$$

by

$$
\mathsf{st}_{A,L}(a,t)
=
\mathsf{map}(\lambda x.(a,x))(t).
$$

This is well typed by FE-007.

### Theorem FE-010 — Strong graded monad

The maps $\mathsf{st}_{A,L}$ are natural in $A,X$, coherent with products, and
compatible with graded unit, bind, and weakening. Hence $\widehat T$ is a strong
$Q$-graded monad.

### Proof

Each strength diagram reduces to a functor or monad law for the underlying tree
monad. Weakening compatibility is immediate because strength does not change
the tree's trace or its refinement witness. $\square$

## 9. Primitive algebraic operations

For $\beta:P\to R$ define

$$
\beta^{\widehat T}(p)
=
\mathsf{base}_\beta
(p,\lambda r.\mathsf{ret}(r)).
$$

Its sole path grade is $|\beta|$, so

$$
\beta^{\widehat T}:
P\to\widehat T_{J(|\beta|)}R.
$$

Similarly,

$$
\operatorname{op}^{\widehat T}_\Delta:
P_{\operatorname{op}}
\to
\widehat T_{J(\Delta)}R_{\operatorname{op}}.
$$

## 10. First-free-factor transformer

Every $m\in M$ has one of these forms relative to $\Delta$:

1. $m=b$ is base-only;
2. $m=b\Delta e$ and the displayed $\Delta$ is the first free factor;
3. $m=b\Gamma e$, $\Gamma\neq\Delta$, and $\Gamma$ is first.

For a clause grade $K\in Q$, define

$$
\phi_{\Delta,K}(b)=\{b\},
$$

$$
\phi_{\Delta,K}(b\Delta e)
=
\{bke\mid k\in K\},
$$

$$
\phi_{\Delta,K}(b\Gamma e)
=
\{b\Gamma e\}
\quad(\Gamma\neq\Delta).
$$

Then

$$
\Phi_{\Delta,K}(L)
=
\downarrow
\bigcup_{m\in L}
\phi_{\Delta,K}(m).
$$

### Lemma FE-011

$\Phi$ is monotone in both arguments:

$$
L\subseteq L',\quad K\subseteq K'
\Longrightarrow
\Phi_{\Delta,K}(L)
\subseteq
\Phi_{\Delta,K'}(L').
$$

### Proof

The union index grows with $L$. In the matching case, the replacement choices
grow with $K$; the other cases are independent of $K$. Downward closure is
monotone. $\square$

### Lemma FE-012 — Base-prefix law

For $b\in B$ and $m\in M$,

$$
\phi_{\Delta,K}(bm)
=
b\,\phi_{\Delta,K}(m),
$$

where $bS=\{bs\mid s\in S\}$.

### Proof

Multiplying by a base factor changes only the initial base component of the
alternating normal form; it cannot introduce a free factor. Inspect the three
cases defining $\phi$. $\square$

## 11. Exhaustive shallow handler

For each operation in $\Delta$, assume a clause

$$
c_{\operatorname{op}}:
P_{\operatorname{op}}
\to
\widehat T_KR_{\operatorname{op}}.
$$

Define $H_\Delta^c$ by well-founded recursion:

$$
H_\Delta^c(\mathsf{ret}(x))=\mathsf{ret}(x),
$$

$$
H_\Delta^c(\mathsf{base}_\beta(p,k))
=
\mathsf{base}_\beta
(p,\lambda r.H_\Delta^c(k(r))),
$$

$$
H_\Delta^c
(\mathsf{free}_{\Delta,\operatorname{op}}(p,k))
=
c_{\operatorname{op}}(p)
\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}
k,
$$

$$
H_\Delta^c
(\mathsf{free}_{\Gamma,\operatorname{op}}(p,k))
=
\mathsf{free}_{\Gamma,\operatorname{op}}(p,k)
\quad(\Gamma\neq\Delta).
$$

The last two equations contain no recursive call to $H_\Delta^c$.

### Lemma FE-013 — Handler path characterization

Every complete output path of $H_\Delta^c(t)$ is obtained from a complete input
path $m$ by:

- leaving it unchanged if it is base-only or its first free factor differs from
  $\Delta$;
- replacing its first $\Delta$ factor by a clause path whose returned operation
  result selects the corresponding continuation subtree, followed by a path of
  that subtree.

### Proof

Tree induction. At a base node, apply the induction hypothesis and FE-012. At a
matching free node, FE-008 concatenates a clause path with precisely the
continuation subtree selected by the clause's returned operation result. At
another free node, the tree is unchanged. $\square$

### Theorem FE-014 — Handler grading theorem

$$
\boxed{
H_{\Delta,L,K}^c:
\widehat T_LX
\to
\widehat T_{\Phi_{\Delta,K}(L)}X.
}
$$

### Proof

If $\mathsf{Tr}(t)\subseteq L$, FE-013 places every output path in
$\bigcup_{m\in L}\phi_{\Delta,K}(m)$, hence in its downward closure. $\square$

## 12. Naturality and clause weakening

### Lemma FE-015 — Naturality in results

For $f:X\to Y$,

$$
\mathsf{map}(f)\circ H_{\Delta,X}^c
=
H_{\Delta,Y}^c\circ\mathsf{map}(f).
$$

### Proof

Tree induction. The matching case uses

$$
\mathsf{map}(f)(u\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}k)
=
u\mathbin{\mathsf{bind}}
(\lambda r.\mathsf{map}(f)(k(r))),
$$

which follows from bind associativity. $\square$

If clauses initially have grades $K_{\operatorname{op}}$, take

$$
K=\bigcup_{\operatorname{op}\in\Delta}K_{\operatorname{op}}.
$$

Weakening each clause to $K$ is proof-irrelevant, and FE-011 shows that further
clause widening only widens the handler result.

## 13. Initiality of the ungraded tree monad

Let $S$ be an ordinary monad equipped with algebraic interpretations of every
base and free operation. Then there is a unique monad morphism

$$
\mathsf{fold}_S:\mathsf{GTree}\Rightarrow S
$$

preserving every primitive operation.

### Theorem FE-016 — Free-monad theorem

$\mathsf{GTree}$ is the free monad on the combined polynomial signature
$\Sigma$.

### Proof

Existence is well-founded recursion:

$$
\mathsf{fold}_S(\mathsf{ret}(x))=\eta^S(x),
$$

and an operation node is interpreted by the corresponding algebraic operation
of $S$, followed by Kleisli extension of the recursively folded continuation.

Monad-morphism laws follow by tree induction. Any operation-preserving monad
morphism must satisfy these same equations, so uniqueness follows by
well-founded induction. $\square$

This initiality is stated for the underlying monad. A grade-preserving fold
requires the target to interpret the trace-language grades and is handled
separately by the typed interpretation theorem.

## 14. Free extension theorem

### Theorem FE-017

Under the assumptions of Section 1, the combined base/free signature induces:

1. the unital quantale $Q=\mathsf{Down}(B*\mathcal D^*)$;
2. a strong $Q$-graded monad $\widehat T$ of well-founded interaction trees;
3. coherent proof-irrelevant subeffecting by inclusion;
4. primitive base and free operations at principal grades;
5. for every interface $\Delta$ and exhaustive clause family of common grade
   $K$, a natural one-shot shallow handler

   $$
   H_{\Delta,L,K}:
   \widehat T_LX
   \to
   \widehat T_{\Phi_{\Delta,K}(L)}X;
   $$

6. a free-monad universal property after forgetting grades.

### Proof

Combine FE-003, FE-009, FE-010, the primitive constructions of Section 9,
FE-014, FE-015, and FE-016. $\square$

## 15. What this theorem does not assert

- $H_\Delta$ is not claimed to be a monad morphism. A shallow handler changes
  only the first free head and intentionally does not commute with arbitrary
  sequencing in the way a deep homomorphic handler would.
- A result grade need not be principal. $\Phi$ can produce a genuine join of
  ordered traces.
- The theorem does not identify the base-only tree carrier with an arbitrary
  pre-existing graded monad $T$.
- Recursion/divergence requires a coinductive or domain-theoretic extension.

These are boundaries of the statement, not missing cases of its proof.
